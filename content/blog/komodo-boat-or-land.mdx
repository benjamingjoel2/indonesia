Komodo National Park covers a scatter of dry, steep islands between Sumbawa and Flores, along with some of the richest reefs in the Coral Triangle. Almost every trip starts in Labuan Bajo, the harbour town on the western tip of Flores, about an hour's flight from Bali. From there, you have two broad choices: live aboard a boat for a few nights, or stay on land and head out on day trips.

## The case for a boat

Sleeping on board is the classic way to see the park, and for good reason. The main sights are spread out, and a boat lets you be in the right place at the right time.

On a two to four night cruise, a typical rhythm looks like this: a pre-dawn climb up **Padar** for the view across its three curved bays, a guided walk with rangers to see Komodo dragons on Rinca or Komodo island, snorkelling at the manta cleaning stations, and a stop at the pink-tinged sand of one of the park's beaches. In the evening, you anchor in a sheltered bay and watch thousands of flying foxes leave the mangroves of Kalong island at dusk.

The boat most people picture is a **phinisi**, the traditional two-masted wooden schooner built by Bugis shipwrights in Sulawesi. They range from simple boats with shared bathrooms to beautifully finished private yachts with ensuite cabins, a chef and dive instructors.

A boat suits you if:

- You want sunrise and sunset in the park rather than a commute each way.
- You enjoy snorkelling or diving and want several sites a day.
- You are a group or family that can charter a whole boat.
- You are comfortable with a compact cabin and some motion at sea.

## The case for staying on land

Labuan Bajo has changed a great deal in the last decade. There are now good hotels and a handful of very comfortable resorts along the coast, some with their own jetties. From here, a private speedboat can reach Padar, the dragon islands and the main snorkelling sites in a long day.

Staying on land works well if anyone in your party is prone to seasickness, if you are travelling with young children, or if you simply prefer a large room and a proper bathroom at the end of the day. It also offers more flexibility: if the weather turns, you can switch days around without losing a night on board.

The trade-off is time. Day trips start early and return late, and you will share the most popular sites with other boats in the middle of the day.

## A middle path

Many of our guests combine the two: one or two nights on a phinisi, followed by a night or two in a resort near Labuan Bajo. You get the experience of waking up at anchor, without committing to a longer cruise. It also gives a buffer before your flight out, which is useful in a region where boat timings can shift with the tides.

## Practical notes

A few things worth knowing before you choose:

- **Season matters.** April to November is generally the best time, with calmer seas. December to March can bring rough crossings and some boats pause for maintenance.
- **Private charters book early.** The better phinisi often sell out nine to twelve months ahead for July and August.
- **Park fees and regulations change.** Entry fees and visitor rules in the national park have been revised several times in recent years, and we include current fees in your quote.
- **Dragons are wild animals.** Walks are always led by park rangers, and you will be briefed on safety before setting off.

## Our view

If you have three or more days and are comfortable on the water, a well-chosen boat is the most memorable way to see Komodo. If you have less time, or comfort on land matters more, a good hotel and a private speedboat will still show you the essentials. Your specialist can talk you through specific boats and hotels, and the honest pros and cons of each.
