Raja Ampat, off the northwest tip of New Guinea in the province of Southwest Papua, is made up of four main islands and more than a thousand smaller ones. It sits at the heart of the Coral Triangle, and its reefs hold a remarkable share of the world's known coral and reef fish species. It is also remote, and that shapes almost every decision about a first trip.

## When to go

The most reliable months are **October to April**, when seas are generally calm and most boats and lodges run their full schedules. From roughly June to September, southeasterly winds can make longer crossings uncomfortable, particularly in the south around Misool. Some liveaboards move to other parts of Indonesia during these months.

Water temperatures stay warm all year, typically 27 to 30°C, so a 3mm wetsuit or a rash guard is usually enough.

## Getting there

There are no international flights into Raja Ampat. The usual route is:

1. Fly into Jakarta, Bali or Makassar.
2. Take a domestic flight to **Sorong**, often with a connection, usually around four to six hours of flying from Bali.
3. Transfer by ferry or private boat to Waisai, the main town on Waigeo, or directly to your lodge or boat.

Because flight schedules change and connections can be tight, we strongly recommend a night in Sorong or a generous buffer on both ends. Southern lodges near Misool can be four or more hours by boat from Sorong.

## Liveaboard or lodge

Most visitors stay in one of two ways.

A **liveaboard**, often a phinisi, moves between regions over seven to twelve nights. It is the best way to see both the northern reefs around the Dampier Strait and the soft coral walls of Misool in the south. It suits keen divers and those happy to spend most of their time on the water.

A **resort or eco-lodge** gives you a fixed base with a house reef, guided dives or snorkelling, and more space. Some are simple homestays run by local families; others are comfortable lodges with private bungalows. Lodges suit non-divers, mixed groups and anyone who wants a quieter rhythm.

## Diving and snorkelling

Raja Ampat is famous for diving, but snorkellers see a great deal too. Many reefs start in very shallow water, and some lodges have excellent reefs right off the jetty. Currents can be strong in places, particularly in the Dampier Strait, so the more demanding sites are best for experienced divers. Guided dives are standard practice here.

Beyond the reefs, the region offers kayaking among karst islets, the viewpoint over **Piaynemo**, and birdwatching for the red bird-of-paradise in the forests of Waigeo.

## Costs and permits

Every visitor must buy a Raja Ampat marine park entry permit, which supports conservation and local communities. Fees have changed in recent years, so we include the current amount in your quote. Overall, Raja Ampat is one of the more expensive regions of Indonesia to visit, largely because of flights, boat fuel and the logistics of supplying remote islands.

## What to pack

A few things are hard to find once you arrive:

- Any prescription medicine, plus a basic first aid kit and seasickness tablets.
- Reef-safe sunscreen and a long-sleeved rash guard.
- Your own mask, if you have one that fits well.
- Enough cash in rupiah for tips and small purchases, as card machines are rare.
- A dry bag for boat transfers.

## Our advice for a first visit

Give it time. With the travel involved, we suggest at least six nights in Raja Ampat itself, and ideally more. Pair it with a few days in Bali at either end to break up the journey and allow for flight changes. Most of all, go with modest expectations of comfort and very high expectations of the sea.
