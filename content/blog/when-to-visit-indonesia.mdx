The most common question we hear is a simple one: when is the best time to go? The honest answer is that it depends on where you want to be. Indonesia stretches more than 5,000 kilometres from west to east, and while most of the islands visitors know follow one weather pattern, a few important ones follow the opposite.

## The two seasons, in broad terms

Across Bali, Lombok, Java, Komodo and Sumba, the year divides into a dry season and a wet season.

The **dry season** runs roughly from April to October. Skies are clearer, humidity is lower, and the seas between the islands are generally calmer. July and August are the busiest months, driven by European and Australian school holidays, and prices for the best villas and boats rise accordingly.

The **wet season** runs roughly from November to March, with the heaviest rain usually in January and February. It rarely rains all day. A typical pattern is a bright morning, a build-up of cloud after lunch and a heavy downpour in the late afternoon or evening. Rice terraces are at their greenest, hotels are quieter and rates are often lower.

The months either side, April to June and September to October, are what we call the shoulder season. For most travellers they offer the best balance of weather, prices and space.

## Bali and Lombok

Bali is a year-round destination, which is part of its appeal. The dry season suits beach time in the south and on the Gili islands, and it is the better choice for climbing Mount Batur or Rinjani on Lombok, which closes to trekkers during the wettest months. In the wet season, the highlands around Ubud and Sidemen are lush and atmospheric, and it is still possible to fill most days.

Look out for **Nyepi**, the Balinese Day of Silence, usually in March. The whole island, including the airport, shuts down for 24 hours. It is a remarkable thing to experience, but it needs planning.

## Komodo and Flores

Komodo National Park is best from April to November. The hills turn gold through the dry months, the seas are kinder for phinisi cruising, and manta encounters are good through much of the season. December to March brings rougher seas and some boat operators pause for maintenance. Trips are possible, but itineraries need to stay flexible.

## Raja Ampat

Raja Ampat in West Papua works the other way round. The calmest seas usually come from **October to April**, which is when most liveaboards and dive lodges operate their full schedules. From June to September, southeasterly winds can make crossings rough, particularly in the south around Misool. Diving is possible year-round, but for a first visit we nearly always suggest the northern hemisphere winter.

## Java

Central and East Java follow the main dry season. Borobudur and Prambanan are best visited from May to October, when sunrise views are more likely to be clear. Mount Bromo and Ijen are accessible for most of the year, though cloud and rain in the wet season reduce the odds of a clear crater view. Visiting during Ramadan is perfectly possible, but some restaurants close during the day and travel around Eid is very busy.

## Sumba

Sumba is drier than its neighbours and has a long dry season from around April to November. The savannah turns golden by August. The Pasola ritual festivals in West Sumba take place in February and March, with dates set by local elders each year.

## How to choose your dates

A few practical points we discuss with almost every guest:

- If you are combining Komodo and Raja Ampat, aim for October, November or April, when both are usually in good condition.
- If you can only travel in July or August, book six to nine months ahead, especially for private boats and small hotels.
- If you want value and space, consider May, June or September for Bali, Java and Komodo.
- If you are travelling in the wet season, build in some flexibility around boat crossings and volcano treks.
- Check Indonesian public holidays and school holidays, which fill domestic flights quickly.

There is rarely a bad time to visit Indonesia, only better and worse times for a particular combination of places. Tell us where you would like to go and when you can travel, and we will be honest about what to expect.
