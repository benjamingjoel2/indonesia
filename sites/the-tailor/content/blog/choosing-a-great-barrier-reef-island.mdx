The Great Barrier Reef runs for more than 2,300 kilometres along the Queensland coast, and the island you stay on decides a great deal: how close the coral is, how easy it is to reach, and how many other people you will share it with. There is no single right answer, only the right fit for how you like to travel. Here is how we help clients choose.

## What to decide first

Before comparing islands, it helps to answer a few questions:

- Do you want to snorkel straight from the beach, or are you happy to take a boat to the outer reef?
- Is the journey part of the pleasure, or would you prefer to arrive with minimal fuss?
- Are you travelling as a couple, with older children or with young children?
- Do you want an all-inclusive stay, or the freedom of several restaurants?

The answers usually narrow the field to one or two places.

## Islands on the reef itself

Most islands described as "on the reef" are in fact continental islands near the coast, with fringing coral but some distance from the main outer reef. A few are genuinely surrounded by it. Lizard Island, in the far north, is the best known: a national park island reached by a light-aircraft flight of about an hour from Cairns, with reef close to its many beaches and day trips to the outer ribbon reefs. It suits couples and families with older children who prioritise snorkelling and diving above all.

Further south, small coral cays such as Lady Elliot Island and Heron Island sit right on the reef. Accommodation there is comfortable rather than luxurious, but the wildlife is remarkable, including turtle nesting from around November to March and, at Lady Elliot, manta rays for much of the year.

## The Whitsundays

The 74 Whitsunday Islands lie between the mainland and the outer reef. Their appeal is the setting: forested islands, sheltered water and, on Whitsunday Island, the fine white silica sand of Whitehaven Beach and Hill Inlet. Hamilton Island has an airport with direct flights from several capital cities, which makes it one of the easiest places to reach. At its northern tip, qualia offers adults-only pavilions with views across the Whitsunday Passage.

Coral here is generally reached by boat or seaplane, typically to the outer reef or to Heart Reef, which is best seen from the air. The Whitsundays suit travellers who enjoy sailing, beaches and a degree of polish, with the reef as one part of the stay rather than the whole of it.

## Staying on the mainland

Port Douglas and the Daintree are an alternative to an island altogether. Boats leave daily for the outer reef, including the Agincourt Reefs, and the Daintree Rainforest is on the doorstep. For families and anyone who enjoys variety, a few nights at a rainforest lodge such as Silky Oaks, combined with a private boat charter to the reef, can be more rewarding than a week on a single island.

## Timing your visit

Reef conditions vary through the year. September to December often brings calm seas and good visibility. From around November to May, marine stingers are present in tropical waters, and a lightweight stinger suit is recommended for swimming; most resorts supply them. Winter, from June to August, is mild and clear, though trade winds can make crossings to the outer reef choppier.

## Our general advice

For a first visit, we often recommend combining two quite different experiences: three or four nights somewhere with reef close at hand, and a few nights in the rainforest or the Whitsundays. Moving between them by seaplane or helicopter adds a view of the reef from above, which is one of the clearest ways to understand its scale. Your Travel Designer can match the islands to your interests, your dates and the age of anyone travelling with you.
