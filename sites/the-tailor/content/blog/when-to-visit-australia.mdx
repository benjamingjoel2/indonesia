The most common question we hear is a simple one: when should we go? The honest answer is that it depends on where. Australia reaches from the tropics, a few hundred kilometres from New Guinea, down to latitudes where the next landfall is Antarctica. When it is humid and stormy in Darwin, it can be crisp and clear in Hobart. Good timing is less about picking a month for the country and more about choosing the right regions for the month you have.

## The tropical north: think wet and dry

Across the top of the country, including Tropical North Queensland, the Top End and the Kimberley, the year divides into two seasons rather than four. The dry season runs roughly from May to October. Skies are reliably clear, humidity is lower and roads into places such as Kakadu and the Gibb River Road are open. This is when most travellers visit, and it is when the best lodges book out first.

The wet season, from about November to April, brings heat, heavy afternoon storms and the possibility of cyclones. Some remote lodges close, and parts of Kakadu become inaccessible by road. Yet the landscape is at its most alive: waterfalls run hard, the rainforest is vivid, and you will share it with far fewer people. With flexible planning, and often a scenic flight in place of a drive, the wet can be rewarding.

## The Great Barrier Reef

The reef can be visited all year, but conditions vary. From June to October the water is clearer and the weather mild, though trade winds can make crossings choppy. September to December often brings the calmest seas and excellent visibility. Water temperatures rise in summer, which is pleasant for swimming but coincides with the stinger season, roughly November to May, when a lightweight stinger suit is sensible protection. Humpback whales pass through the Whitsundays from around June to September.

## The Red Centre

Uluru, Kata Tjuta and the MacDonnell Ranges are best from April to September. Days are warm and dry, nights are cool, and in mid-winter can drop close to freezing, which makes for spectacular star-filled skies. In summer, daytime temperatures regularly pass 40°C, and walks such as the Valley of the Winds may close by late morning for safety. If summer is your only window, we plan activities around sunrise and sunset.

## The south: Tasmania, Kangaroo Island and the wine country

The southern states are at their most comfortable from October to April. Spring brings wildflowers and newborn wildlife; summer has long evenings and warm days; autumn, from March to May, is our quiet favourite, with settled weather, vintage in the wine regions and golden light. Winter in the south is cool and sometimes wet, but it is also when fires are lit, lodges are calm and the Southern Right whales arrive off South Australia's coast.

## A simple way to plan

When we build a journey that covers several regions, we usually start with the one that has the strictest season and arrange the others around it. A few broad pairings work especially well:

- **April to May:** the Red Centre, Kangaroo Island and the Barossa in autumn colour.
- **June to August:** the Top End, the reef and the Red Centre, all at their best together.
- **September to October:** the reef in calm conditions, the Kimberley before the heat, and Tasmania in early spring.
- **November to March:** Tasmania, Kangaroo Island, Sydney and the southern wine regions.

## School holidays and peak periods

Australian school holidays, along with Christmas, New Year and Easter, fill domestic flights and the most sought-after lodges quickly. If your dates overlap with these periods, it helps to begin planning nine to twelve months ahead. Outside them, three to six months is often enough, though the smallest lodges, some with only a handful of rooms, reward earlier planning at any time of year.
