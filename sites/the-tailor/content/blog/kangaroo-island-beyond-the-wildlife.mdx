Kangaroo Island sits about 15 kilometres off the South Australian coast, a short flight from Adelaide, and it is often described simply as a wildlife destination. That is fair as far as it goes. You can watch Australian sea lions resting on the beach at Seal Bay, see kangaroos grazing at dusk and, with patience, find echidnas and koalas. But the island is also about 150 kilometres long, with a coastline, a food culture and a story of recovery that deserve more than a day trip.

## A landscape in recovery

The bushfires of 2019–20 burned close to half of the island, including much of Flinders Chase National Park on its western end. The recovery since has been striking. Eucalypts have resprouted from their trunks, grass trees have flowered in profusion, and walking tracks and visitor facilities in the park have been rebuilt. Seeing this regeneration first-hand, ideally with a local guide who knows what was lost and what has returned, is one of the most moving parts of a visit.

## The coast

The south and west coasts face the open Southern Ocean, and the scale is dramatic. In Flinders Chase, Remarkable Rocks is a cluster of wind-carved granite boulders on a dome above the sea, and nearby Admirals Arch is a natural rock arch where New Zealand fur seals rest on the ledges below. On the north coast, the water is calmer, with sheltered beaches such as Stokes Bay, reached through a short passage among the rocks, and Snelling Beach, a wide sweep of sand between green hills.

## Food and drink

The island has a strong producer culture for its size. Ligurian bees, introduced in the nineteenth century, are protected here, and the island's honey is prized. You can taste sheep's milk cheese, gin made from local botanicals, oysters from the bays and wines from a number of small cellar doors. A long lunch at a farm or winery is one of the island's quieter pleasures, and our Travel Designers often build a day around it.

Highlights we include regularly:

- A morning at Seal Bay, walking the boardwalk or joining a ranger on the beach
- Time in Flinders Chase at Remarkable Rocks and Admirals Arch
- A farm or producer visit with lunch, often focused on honey, cheese or gin
- A dusk drive to watch kangaroos and other wildlife come out to feed
- A swim at a sheltered north coast beach in the warmer months

## Where to stay

Southern Ocean Lodge, rebuilt after the fires and reopened in late 2023, is the island's most celebrated stay. It sits on the clifftops above Hanson Bay, close to Flinders Chase, and its all-inclusive stays include guided experiences across the island. There are also smaller, well-designed retreats and private houses around the coast, which suit families or those who prefer self-contained space.

## How long and when

We suggest three nights at a minimum. The island is larger than most people expect, and roads in some areas are unsealed, so the pace is unhurried by necessity. Flights from Adelaide to Kingscote take around 30 minutes; there is also a vehicle ferry from Cape Jervis, about two hours' drive south of Adelaide.

Spring and autumn are our favourite seasons. From September to November, wildflowers bloom and many animals have young; from March to May, the weather is settled and the light soft. Summer is warm and busier, and winter brings dramatic seas and cosy evenings by the fire.

Kangaroo Island pairs naturally with the Barossa, McLaren Vale or the Adelaide Hills, and with a few nights in Adelaide itself. Taken together, they make one of the most rewarding regional journeys in the country.
